# Choosing a baseline for voltammetric peak-height calibration

Use this guide before setting `baseline.windows` in the manifest. It applies to a **peak-to-baseline** response; it does not turn an unresolved shoulder, overlapping process, or drifting blank into a validated analyte peak. The current runner supports only one-window constant and two-window straight baselines. More complex corrections require a separately defined, validated method.

## Evidence and its limits

- IUPAC defines differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) as a peak-shaped difference between two sampled currents. This reduces the influence of charging current; it does not establish a zero or flat background. [Pingarrón et al., *Pure and Applied Chemistry* 92 (2020) 641–694, DOI: 10.1515/pac-2018-0109](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2018-0109).
- Bond and Grabaric fitted a quadratic to DPV and related polarographic data **excluding the faradaic peak** to predict curved background current. This supports peak-free fitting regions and a validated curved alternative when a straight line is inadequate; it does not prescribe quadratic correction for every sample. [*Analytical Chemistry* 51 (1979) 337–341, DOI: 10.1021/ac50039a005](https://doi.org/10.1021/ac50039a005).
- Górski, Ciepiela, and Jakubowska showed that voltammetric residual current varies with electrode, matrix, oxygen, impurities, and conditions, and validated an iterative polynomial correction with simulated signals and certified reference materials. Do not assume one stored blank or one baseline shape works across a series. [*Electrochimica Acta* 136 (2014) 195–203, DOI: 10.1016/j.electacta.2014.05.076](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.electacta.2014.05.076).
- In **square-wave** Fe/Mn measurements in seawater, Slowey and Marvin-DiPasquale found that linear baselines and a common blank could bias calibration when background was curved or processes overlapped. Moving reasonable anchors by 10 mV changed a weak peak by about ±30% **in that system**. Their voltage offsets, polynomial degree, and ±30% are examples, not universal DPV settings. [*Geochemical Transactions* 13 (2012) 6, DOI: 10.1186/1467-4866-13-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/1467-4866-13-6).
- CV return peaks may require a different physical background model: simple linear extrapolation underestimated return peak current in the cases studied by Macedo and coauthors. Do not transplant a DPV flank rule to a CV return branch without checking its physics. [*Analytical Chemistry* 96 (2024) 1530–1537, DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04181](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04181).

The skill's numerical peak-quality limits (SNR 5, prominence fraction 0.25, peak-position shift 0.03 V) are **workflow screening settings**, not thresholds prescribed by these papers.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify technique, scan branch, sign convention, analyte redox region, and any analyte-free blank. Overlay all **unsmoothed** raw curves. Find the full potential span of the candidate peak from the stronger signals, then inspect weak signals and the blank at the same potentials. Never move a search window by concentration to create aligned peaks.
2. Mark potential intervals immediately before and after the entire peak that contain only background over the series. Reject an interval if it contains an analyte tail, neighboring redox feature, onset/step, switching transient, or unstable electrode/matrix background. Stay close enough to the peak that a straight line would be local interpolation, not long extrapolation. Use multiple original points per anchor when sampling permits; the current helper's minimum of two is a parsing minimum, not evidence of an adequate baseline.
3. Choose **constant** only for demonstrably flat local background over the relevant interval. Choose **linear** only when both usable flanks support an approximately straight local background. Compare the flank trends and show the proposed baseline over every raw curve. If curvature, overlap, or missing flanks makes either model implausible, stop the standard helper and define a separate validated curved-baseline, blank-subtraction, or peak-resolution method. Do not choose a higher polynomial order merely because it raises calibration R².
4. Inspect the corrected curves at the peak and both shoulders. A plausible analyte response is internal to the common search window, maintains chemically sensible potential alignment, and separates visibly from background; the corrected shoulders should approach their local background. A blank is a **background control**, not an analyte peak that must be made positive. Preserve its signal and uncertainty, and explicitly state whether it enters the calibration. A blank-like peak at the analyte potential requires investigating interference, not silently setting it to zero.
5. Check anchor sensitivity **without optimizing the fit**: predefine one or more small alternative placements (for example one potential sample step) that remain within independently identified background-only regions. Recompute candidate peak heights with the same alternative rule for all concentrations. Record the change in each response and, if a validated fit exists, in K. Compare it with repeatability or the accuracy needed for the task; there is no universal percentage tolerance. If results depend materially on an equally plausible placement, report baseline ambiguity and seek better data or a validated alternative instead of selecting the placement with the best R².
6. In the case folder, save an overlay plot showing raw curves, peak interval, both anchor intervals, and fitted baseline; record exact potentials, point counts, selected model, blank handling, why the flanks are background-only, and the sensitivity check in `baseline_choice.md`. Preserve the primary rule even if another plausible rule gives a prettier calibration.

For blank subtraction, verify that the blank and samples share electrode state, matrix, waveform, and acquisition timing; inspect whether the background shape remains stable. Blank subtraction and per-curve local baseline estimation are different estimands. If the protocol does not identify which response to calibrate, ask rather than switching after a quality failure.
